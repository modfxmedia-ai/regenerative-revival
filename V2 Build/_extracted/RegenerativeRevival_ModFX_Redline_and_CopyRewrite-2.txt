Part 2: Critical Compliance Issue
The single highest-priority item in this review is below. Nothing else proceeds until this is fixed.
CRITICAL — Product Label: "For Research Use Only / Not for Human Use" MUST BE REMOVED
The Semaglutide product mockup shows a bottle label reading "For Research Use Only / Not for Human Use." This is the exact language used by gray-market and non-prescription peptide vendors to dodge FDA enforcement. It directly contradicts our physician-prescribed, Arora-overseen telehealth model and creates several serious problems:
•  It misrepresents the product. Our Semaglutide is a physician-prescribed compounded medication, not a research chemical.
•  It will fail Arora Health Group compliance review. Arora is the prescribing medical group; they will not allow this language on a product they are clinically responsible for.
•  It exposes Regenerative Revival to FDA enforcement risk, state pharmacy board complaints, and potential cease-and-desist letters from compounding pharmacy partners.
•  It undermines patient trust. Anyone who has done five minutes of research recognizes this phrase as gray-market peptide language.

Required label text (replace with):
•  REGENERATIVE REVIVAL
•  SUBLINGUAL SEMAGLUTIDE
•  Compounded by [Pharmacy Name]
•  5 mg/mL · 30 mL
•  Rx Only — Dispensed pursuant to a valid prescription

This is a hard blocker. The product page cannot go to staging or any client review with the current label.

Part 3: Design Direction Decisions
3A. Home Page Hero — Recommendation: GO WITH PROTOTYPE 3 (Node 209:15)
Why
The human body silhouette + health data overlay aesthetic positions Regenerative Revival as a longevity-tech company, not a generic wellness brand. The gradient/bokeh hero in Prototype 1 is visually overused across the wellness space (every Instagram supplement brand uses it).
Prototype 3 includes the logo in the top left, the "Patient Portal" CTA, and a Blog link — all of which we want long-term.
"50+ States Covered" is the correct number (Arora is licensed in all 50). Prototype 1’s "12+ States Covered" is wrong and needs to be fixed.
Refinements needed on the body + data overlay
The numeric noise strings ("2323234763 8799876544") look like placeholder lorem ipsum. Replace with brand-relevant data points such as: biomarker labels (Testosterone, NAD+, hs-CRP, HbA1c), peptide categories (Recovery, Cognition, Metabolic), or age-reversal indicators. Make the data feel intentional, not random.
Keep the red upward-trending graph — it reads as "longevity gains," which is on-brand.
The "SCAN 3468" indicator can stay if it points to a meaningful body region; otherwise replace with a labeled callout (e.g., "Joint Health" or "Hormone Optimization").
Logo should remain top-left in white.
3B. Product Page Colors — Recommendation: GO WITH PROTOTYPE 4 (Navy CTA, Magenta Nav)
Why
Navy primary CTA adds clinical credibility. Pure purple-on-white in Prototype 2 reads dated and homogenous.
Magenta accent on the nav button adds modern energy and breaks up the palette.
Refinement
Make sure magenta is used sparingly (nav CTA only, not throughout the page). Overuse of magenta will fight the men’s TRT/peptide positioning. Keep the rest of the page primarily navy + purple + white.

SECTION 1 BODY:
Semaglutide is a GLP-1 receptor agonist — a class of medications originally developed for type 2 diabetes and now widely used for weight management. It works by signaling fullness, slowing gastric emptying, and reducing food noise and cravings. Most clinical research has been done on injectable forms. Compounded sublingual semaglutide delivers the same active molecule under the tongue, where it is absorbed through the oral mucosa. For patients who don’t tolerate injections or prefer a daily oral dose, sublingual is a practical alternative — when prescribed and monitored by a clinician.

Section 2 — Who Sublingual Semaglutide Is For
•  Adults with a BMI of 27+ (with comorbidities) or 30+
•  People who have struggled with appetite regulation despite diet and exercise
•  Patients who don’t want to self-inject
•  Anyone seeking a clinician-guided weight loss program rather than a DIY approach

Section 6 — Frequently Asked Questions
Q: Is sublingual semaglutide as effective as injectable?
A: Both forms deliver the same active molecule. Injectable semaglutide has more published clinical data. Sublingual is a practical alternative for patients who prefer not to inject, and many patients see meaningful results — but your provider will help you decide which form is right for you.

Q: How quickly will I see results?
A: Most patients begin noticing reduced appetite within the first 1–2 weeks and meaningful weight loss within 4–8 weeks. Sustainable change takes 3–6 months.

Q: Do I need lab work?
A: Yes. Baseline labs are typically required before starting, and follow-up labs may be ordered depending on your protocol. We coordinate this through our national lab network.

Q: What are the side effects?
A: Common side effects include nausea, reduced appetite, and mild GI changes, especially in the first few weeks. Your provider will discuss the full risk profile during your consultation.

Q: Can I combine this with other Regenerative Revival programs?
A: Yes. Many patients pair GLP-1 programs with peptide therapy, hormone optimization, or NAD+ for a coordinated longevity plan.

Q: Is this covered by insurance?
A: No. Compounded GLP-1 programs are typically cash-pay. Pricing is transparent and includes provider consults, prescription, and shipping.
